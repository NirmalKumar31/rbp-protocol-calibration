\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Sequence models for RNA-binding proteins are evaluated against constructed negative windows, and
how those windows are chosen is usually treated as a detail. Across 94 paired ENCODE eCLIP
datasets we held the model, the peak set, the chromosome-blocked folds and the estimator fixed,
varied only how the negatives were built, and measured each model's nested contribution over a
19-feature composition baseline refit under each protocol.

The measured contribution moves 5.42-fold across three protocols for a 4-mer (95\% CI 4.43 to
6.58), and 3.7 to 7.4-fold across three model classes on identical rows. A model's apparent AUROC
moves the opposite way: replacing GC-matched with dinucleotide-matched negatives lowers the
4-mer's own AUROC from a panel mean of 0.798 to 0.688 in all 94 datasets while raising its
measured contribution from 0.027 to 0.066 in 88 of them.

The estimator also has a floor. Applied to a 2-mer, whose true contribution is zero because the
baseline spans its features, it returns $+0.011$ to $+0.014$: 90.4\% of the smallest protocol's
reported value. That floor is not intrinsic to the estimand but to the usual two-stage way of
computing it. Generating the score covariate outside the outer test fold reduces it by at least
95\% in every protocol, to the zero the construction requires, and leaves the 4-mer's protocol
span at 4.84-fold.

In a survey of seven widely used methods and benchmarks, none reports a composition-only AUROC
beside its headline. We recommend cross-fitting the score covariate, reporting a
composition-only AUROC obtained under the same protocol, and not comparing contributions
measured under different protocols.
\end{abstract}
