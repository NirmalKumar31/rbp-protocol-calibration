\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Sequence models for RNA-binding proteins are evaluated against constructed negative windows,
and how those windows are chosen is usually treated as a detail. Across 94 paired ENCODE
eCLIP datasets we held the model, the peak set, the chromosome-blocked folds and the estimator
fixed, varied only how the negatives were built, and measured each model's nested contribution
over a 19-feature composition baseline refit under each protocol. Replacing GC-matched with dinucleotide-matched negatives lowered the 4-mer's own AUROC from a
panel mean of 0.798 to 0.689 and raised its measured contribution from 0.027 to 0.066.
Both changes held in every one of the 94 datasets individually, not only in the panel mean.
The direction is implied by the design,
since dinucleotide matching constrains fifteen degrees of freedom of composition against GC
matching's one, so only the magnitude is informative. A bias-aware protocol
drawing negatives from other proteins' binding sites left the highest composition baseline of
the three and the smallest contribution, 0.012, and there nineteen composition features beat the
4-mer outright in 53 of 94 datasets. It is also the easiest discrimination for the 4-mer and the
convolutional network, though not for SpliceBERT, which finds GC matching marginally easier. Apparent difficulty and measured contribution therefore
move in opposite directions throughout this panel, spanning 5.42-fold for a 4-mer and 3.7 to
7.4-fold across three model classes on identical rows. A fourth construction shows what the
protocol is acting on. Dinucleotide-preserving shuffling makes each negative a permutation of
its own positive, so the composition baseline takes the same value on both members of every pair
and sits at exactly 0.5000 in all 94 datasets; the
contribution there is $+0.2523$ and equals the model's own AUROC less a half. Across the four
constructions the span is 20.62-fold, and that arm's value is an identity rather than a
measurement: with the baseline pinned at a half, the contribution is the model's own AUROC less
a half. What a negative-set protocol moves is the
composition baseline, and the contribution follows. On the one externally constructed
benchmark we tested they move together, so the relation is a property of the protocols compared
here and not a general law. No standard way of re-expressing an AUROC difference closed the span. We tried eight, including
the logit and probit scales, discriminability, and division by the baseline's remaining
headroom, the distance from the baseline AUROC to one; five shrank the span but not below
two-fold, two are controls that return it unchanged by construction, and one widened it. An
exponent fitted so as to close it survived neither a change of aggregation nor transfer to an
independent benchmark. At an order-three baseline most of the 4-mer's contribution disappears and the
span widens, so the quantity is indexed by the baseline's order as well as the protocol. Headroom division improved cross-protocol agreement on our own data but failed, out of sample,
a falsification test specified in advance. The estimator also has a floor. Applied to a model whose information the baseline already
contains, so that the true contribution is zero by construction, it returns $+0.0111$ to
$+0.0137$: nearly constant across protocols, so the span is not manufactured by it, but 90.6\%
of the smallest arm's reported contribution, so absolute increments of this size are not
distinguishable from it. In a survey of seven widely used methods and benchmarks, five construct
negatives by relocating genomic intervals, which leaves composition unconstrained, none reports a
composition-only AUROC beside its headline, and none reports a calibration that would reveal the
floor. We therefore recommend reporting the composition-only AUROC obtained
under the same protocol alongside every headline AUROC, and not comparing contributions measured
under different protocols.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} RNA-binding proteins; eCLIP; benchmarking; negative-set
construction; sequence composition; incremental value; AUROC
