How much a sequence model adds over sequence composition is a joint property of the model and
of the negative set it was evaluated against. Across 94 ENCODE eCLIP datasets, varying only the
construction of the negative windows changed the measured contribution of a 4-mer by a factor of
5.4, and of three model classes by factors between 3.8 and 7.6, while moving in the opposite
direction to apparent performance. The bias-aware protocol proposed to remove compositional and
accessibility artefacts produced the easiest discrimination of the three and the smallest
measured contribution, so a lower score on a more carefully constructed negative set should not
be read as evidence of a more demanding evaluation.

Part of this dependence is arithmetic, and eight standard reparameterisations reduce it. None
removes it, and the exponent that does close the span is fitted rather than chosen: it does not
transport between benchmarks, and it differs between model classes measured on the same data.
There is therefore no protocol-free summary of what a sequence model contributes that can be
computed once and compared across studies.

We recommend that the composition-only AUROC obtained under the same protocol be reported
alongside every headline AUROC, and that contributions measured under different negative-set
protocols not be compared. The first is the only summary of a protocol's effect that a reader
can act on. The second is a restriction we are able to justify, where a normalisation that
removes the need for it is not.
