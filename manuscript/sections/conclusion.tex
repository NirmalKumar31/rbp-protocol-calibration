How much a sequence model adds over sequence composition is a joint property of the model and
of the negative set it was evaluated against, and on this panel the two axes move against each
other: the protocol that made the discrimination easiest by AUROC was also the one that left a
sequence model least to add.

For a benchmark designer this has two operational consequences. Report the composition-only
AUROC obtained under the same protocol alongside every headline AUROC; it costs one logistic
regression on nineteen features and it is the only summary of a protocol's effect that a reader
can act on. And do not compare contributions measured under different negative-set protocols.
That restriction is one we can justify, where a normalisation that would remove the need for it
is not: eight standard reparameterisations reduce the span without closing it, and the exponent
that does close it is fitted rather than chosen, differing between benchmarks and between model
classes measured on the same data.

One further consequence is for how protocols are compared to each other. The three here differ
in what they hold fixed and not only in how tightly they match composition, and the one that
frees transcript region pays for it in a baseline that a nineteen-feature model can read. A
protocol is not made more stringent by being made less constrained.
